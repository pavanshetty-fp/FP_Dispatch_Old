*Shift Planner — what the prototype does today*

Two columns: the orders still to plan on the left, drivers and their trucks on the right. Drag an order onto a truck and it loads. Both columns resize, and either can be popped out into its own window.

*Planning*
• Drag and drop orders onto a rig — fuel packs into compartments, one product per compartment
• Every order card scores itself against the selected truck: "Fills the truck", "Fits · rig 90%", "80% fits · 2,000 gal over". Sort the list by fit.
• Move fuel between compartments, whole or in part
• Overloading is caught and given a way out — cut the biggest order back, or swap it for one that fits the space
• An order planned on another driver can't be taken without releasing it first
• Change the truck or the driver on any lane; hitch and unhitch trailers

*The truck is drawn*
• Each lane shows the real rig with its compartments, what's in each, gallons and fill
• Five classes, from a 2,800-gal bobtail to a 10,500-gal transport, plus trailers

*Drivers*
• 30 drivers, filtered by availability, duty state and AM/PM shift, or searched
• Available holds its order while you work in a lane, so the card you're loading doesn't slide out from under you
• Shift window with time remaining
• *Hours of service from Samsara* — Break, Drive, Shift and Cycle on every lane, plus a detail sheet with the dials, the weekly cycle, the ELD day graph and the duty log. Surfaced, never computed: Samsara runs the rules, we read the numbers.

*Products*
• Splash blends — E10, E15, E85, B5, B20 — with each constituent as its own chip carrying its ratio
• Orders that mix a blend with a straight product

*Scenarios it covers*
• Trucks still in the yard, empty, shift not yet begun
• Trucks already out and part-loaded, and trucks that left with no room at all
• An order too big for the rig, and one that fills it exactly
• A driver whose day looks fine but who is nearly out of weekly cycle hours
• A day's work across 18 ship-tos, 05:00 to 21:25

*Not yet:* lines of service, weight-based capacity, switch-loading, reloads mid-route, and sourcing from inside the planner.

Next step: creating a functional prototype for sourcing of this fuel. After that, the next step would be improving the UI and interaction for these prototypes.
