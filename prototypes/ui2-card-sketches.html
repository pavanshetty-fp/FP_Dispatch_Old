Order card — three directions, in wireframe

Greyscale on purpose. Every grey here stands for something that will carry a token, a weight or a colour later; what is being decided is the order you read things in, not the finish. All three hold exactly the same facts and are drawn at the column's real width, three cards deep, so the rhythm of a list is visible and not just one card in isolation.

Where we are
What the card is doing wrong today
1 — There is no headline figure. The single number that decides everything, the total gallons, is not on the card at all. You have to add three quantities to learn whether an order is big.
2 — Four zones, no reading order. A head row, a left column, a right column and a full-width foot strip, all at roughly one volume. The card's height is set by whichever column happens to be taller.
3 — The products float. They sit in an unlabelled right column with no relationship to anything beside them.
4 — The loudest thing is the least useful. A full-width tinted fit strip across the foot out-shouts the ship-to and the gallons.
5 — Five pills compete — type, status, blend, the window when it clashes, and the fit. Each was added for a good reason; together they are a border.
Three directions
Each takes a different view of what you are doing when you look at this list
A says you are sizing. B says you are reading one order at a time. C says you are comparing twenty-six of them. They are not polish variants of one idea — pick the sentence that is true and the layout follows.
AThe figure leads
The total gallons becomes the card's headline, set against the order number. Everything else drops a level and becomes quiet rows. The fit loses its strip and becomes a line under the figure, where the figure already is.

What I would do
B, with its load band capped
B is the one that reads. It is also the only one of the three where the quantities stay on a single right edge, which is what lets you compare two orders without doing arithmetic — and it already closes with the total, so it needs nothing from A.

Its one real weakness is that the card grows with the order: 165px plain, 215px blended. The fix is to cap the load band — show the first two products and a “+1” that opens the rest in place, so every card is about 165px whatever is on it. That is roughly five on screen, against four today.

C I would keep for later as a density toggle — the same card with its middle band folded away — rather than as the default. A reads well but it buys the headline figure by giving up the aligned quantities, and those are worth more in a list you are picking from.
